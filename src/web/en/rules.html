The rules of President, and the Larbin variant

President — also known as Scum, Asshole or Capitalism — is played by four or more with a 52-card deck, and fits in one idea: get rid of your cards before the others, then make the last ones pay for it in the next round.

What you need

  • 4 to 6 players. Four is the sweet spot: two winners, two losers, no spectators.
  • 52 cards, no jokers.
  • Deal all the cards, one at a time, clockwise. With five or six players some get one card more: it doesn’t matter.

In the very first round nobody has a role yet. The most common convention — and the one this game follows — is that the player holding the queen of hearts leads. After that, the winner of the previous round always starts.

Card ranking

It doesn’t follow the usual order: the 2 is the highest card in the game, and the 3 the lowest.

Suits don’t matter. A 6 of hearts is worth exactly a 6 of clubs. Only the rank is compared — which is why the game is so quick to learn.

How a trick is played

The player who leads plays whatever they like: a single card, a pair, three or four of a kind — always of the same rank. That choice sets the format for the whole trick.

Each player in turn must then play the same number of cards, of a strictly higher rank. Anyone who can’t — or won’t — passes. When nobody goes higher, the last player to have played wins the trick: they clear the pile and lead a new trick with whatever they like.

Example: Anna leads a pair of 7s. Bruno plays a pair of 10s, Chloe a pair of Queens, David passes. Anna has no pair high enough: she passes. Chloe wins the trick and leads again.

Roles, at the end of the round

The round ends when only one player still holds cards: they are last, and the order in which players went out hands out the roles.

The card exchange

This is the heart of the game, and what makes it more than a race. Before the next round:

  • the Lackey gives their two best cards to the Boss, who hands back two cards of their choice — or, in the strict version, their two lowest;
  • the Underling gives their best card to the Deputy, who hands back one card;
  • Neutrals exchange nothing.

So the last player starts the next round handicapped, and the first one stronger. It’s unfair — and that’s exactly the point: the game is called President because it stages a hierarchy that has to be overturned.

Where does the game come from? From Asia. President descends from a family of so-called “climbing” games, the best known being the Japanese Dai Hin Min — “the very poor” — and its mirror Daifugō, “the very rich”, as well as the Chinese Zheng Shangyou, “the race to the top”. The game reached the West in the second half of the 20th century and picked up table names along the way, passed on by word of mouth and rarely written down — which is why there are almost as many rule sets as there are families.

Le Larbin: the one-lap variant

Le Larbin (French for “the lackey”) is a house variant of President. It keeps the ranking, the roles and the exchange, but changes three things — and those three things are enough to make it a different game.

1. A trick goes around the table only once

Each player speaks only once per trick. Neither the one who played nor the one who passed comes back into it. Once the lap is done, the trick ends, and the highest play leads the next one.

Anna plays a 7, Bruno goes up to 9, Chloe passes, David plays a Jack. Done. Anna may well be holding a Queen: she won’t play again in this trick.

The consequence is immediate: you can no longer keep a big card “to come back in later”. There is no later. You play it at the right moment, or you keep it for the next trick. The game becomes much faster-paced, and much less forgiving.

2. The 2 cuts

As soon as a 2 is played, the trick ends on the spot: nothing can go higher, the following players don’t even pass. Whoever cut leads again right away.

But the weapon cuts both ways: finishing your round by playing a 2 makes you the Lackey by default, whatever place you actually finished in. So you cut gladly during the round — never on your last card.

3. No more shuffling

The deck is shuffled only at the very start of the game. After that, at the end of each round, the cards are gathered as they are: in the order they fell, with the last player’s hand placed on top.

The Boss prepares the next round. They cut the deck wherever they like, turn over the top card so the whole table can see it, and keep it. The rest is dealt starting from their left-hand neighbour — so they end up with as many cards as everyone else.

The cut then becomes the only chance left in the game, and it is the privilege of the winner. An attentive player who remembers the order in which the cards fell can even aim.

Exchanges, fixed on both sides

In this variant, nobody chooses: the Lackey gives their two highest cards, the Boss hands back their two lowest, and the same one step down between Underling and Deputy. Since suits are worthless, there is no decision left to make — so the exchange happens by itself, at the start of the round.

Scoring

Each round earns as many points as players you left behind you: with four, 3 for the Boss, 2 for the Deputy, 1 for the Underling, nothing for the Lackey. The game goes to 5 points per opponent — 15 with four players, 20 with five, 25 with six.
